# XANORIDEL (xanoridostat) Prescribing Information, Tenant Pharma_A Confidential

## 0.0 Warnings in Brief
- **SERIOUS SKIN REACTIONS:** Stop Xanoridel at the first sign of a rash. Severe skin reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome, were reported in the first 2 months of treatment.
- **CONTRAINDICATIONS:** Do not use with azathioprine or mercaptopurine.

## 1.1 Indications and Usage
Xanoridel is a xanthine oxidase inhibitor indicated for the chronic lowering of serum uric acid in adults with gout.
- Xanoridel is **NOT** recommended for the treatment of high uric acid without gout (asymptomatic hyperuricemia).
- Xanoridel does not treat a gout flare that has already started.

## 1.2 Limitations of Use
Safety and efficacy have **NOT** been established in patients younger than 18 years, or for the prevention of kidney stones in patients without gout.

## 2.1 Recommended Dosage
The recommended starting dose is **40 mg orally once daily**, with or without food.
- Measure serum uric acid 2 weeks after starting and after each dose change.
- Raise the dose by 40 mg every 2 weeks until the serum uric acid is below 6 mg/dL.

## 2.2 Maximum Dose
The maximum daily dose is **160 mg**. Doses above 160 mg once daily were not studied.

## 2.3 Renal Impairment Dosing
| Creatinine clearance | Starting dose | Maximum dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 60 mL/min or more | 40 mg once daily | 160 mg once daily |
| 30 to 59 mL/min | 40 mg once daily | 80 mg once daily |
| 15 to 29 mL/min | 20 mg once daily | 40 mg once daily |
| Below 15 mL/min or on dialysis | Not recommended | Not recommended |

## 2.4 Hepatic Impairment Dosing
No dose change is needed for mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A). The maximum dose in moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B) is 80 mg once daily. Xanoridel has not been studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) and is not recommended.

## 2.5 Gout Flare Prophylaxis
Gout flares may increase when Xanoridel is started, because urate crystals move out of the joints. Give flare prophylaxis with colchicine or a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug for at least **6 months** after starting Xanoridel. If a flare occurs, do not stop Xanoridel; treat the flare at the same time.

## 2.6 Administration Instructions
Swallow the tablets whole. The 40 mg and 80 mg tablets are scored and may be split. Take Xanoridel at about the same time each day.

## 2.7 Missed Dose
If a dose is missed, take it on the same day. Do not take two doses on the next day.

## 3 Dosage Forms and Strengths
Film-coated tablets: 20 mg (white, round), 40 mg (yellow, oval, scored) and 80 mg (orange, oval, scored).

## 4 Contraindications
Xanoridel is contraindicated in patients:
- treated with azathioprine or mercaptopurine, because xanthine oxidase breaks these medicines down and their levels can rise to toxic levels;
- with a known allergy to xanoridostat or to any ingredient of the tablet;
- who had a serious skin reaction to Xanoridel or to another xanthine oxidase inhibitor.

## 5.1 Serious Skin Reactions
Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis and drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms were reported in 0.1% of patients, most in the first 8 weeks. Patients who carry the HLA-B*58:01 allele had a higher rate. Stop Xanoridel at the first rash and do not restart it.

## 5.2 Cardiovascular Death
In XANO-CV, cardiovascular death occurred in 2.1% of patients on Xanoridel and 1.8% of patients on allopurinol (hazard ratio 1.17; 95% CI 0.88 to 1.55). Weigh this in patients with established cardiovascular disease, and tell them the symptoms of heart attack and stroke.

## 5.3 Liver Injury
Raised liver enzymes above 3 times the upper limit of normal occurred in 1.9% of patients. Check liver tests before starting, at 2 months and when symptoms suggest liver injury. Stop Xanoridel if ALT stays above 3 times the upper limit of normal with a raised bilirubin.

## 5.4 Gout Flares
Flares increased in the first 3 months of treatment: 34% of patients on Xanoridel had a flare in that time, against 22% on placebo. Flare prophylaxis lowers the rate (see 2.5).

## 5.5 Kidney Stones in Tumor Lysis
Xanoridel was not studied in patients with tumor lysis or Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, where xanthine can build up and form kidney stones. It is not recommended in these patients.

## 5.6 Monitoring
Measure serum uric acid 2 weeks after starting and after each dose change, then every 6 months. Measure creatinine clearance before starting and every 12 months.

## 6.1 Clinical Trials Experience
The data below come from 2,840 patients with gout treated for up to 52 weeks in three controlled trials (XANO-1, XANO-2 and XANO-CV).

## 6.2 Common Adverse Reactions
| Adverse reaction | Xanoridel 80 mg (n=1,120) | Xanoridel 160 mg (n=860) | Placebo (n=420) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Gout flare (first 3 months) | 31% | 37% | 22% |
| Raised liver enzymes | 1.4% | 2.6% | 0.7% |
| Diarrhea | 3.1% | 3.8% | 2.4% |
| Rash | 1.8% | 2.2% | 1.0% |
| Headache | 2.9% | 3.0% | 2.6% |
| Joint pain | 2.4% | 2.6% | 2.9% |

## 6.3 Discontinuations
Adverse reactions led 4.6% of patients on Xanoridel 80 mg, 6.9% on 160 mg and 3.1% on placebo to stop treatment. The most common reasons were rash and raised liver enzymes.

## 6.4 Postmarketing Experience
Reports since approval include drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms, interstitial nephritis and low platelet counts. These come from a population of unknown size, so a rate cannot be given.

## 7.1 Azathioprine and Mercaptopurine
Xanoridel raises the levels of azathioprine and mercaptopurine, which can cause severe bone marrow suppression. Use together is contraindicated (see 4).

## 7.2 Theophylline
Xanoridel raised theophylline exposure by 40%. Measure theophylline levels when starting Xanoridel.

## 7.3 Medicines With No Interaction
No dose change is needed with colchicine, naproxen, indomethacin, hydrochlorothiazide, warfarin or desipramine.

## 7.4 Summary of Drug Interactions
| Medicine | Effect | What to do |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Azathioprine | Level rises many times | Contraindicated |
| Mercaptopurine | Level rises many times | Contraindicated |
| Theophylline | Exposure up 40% | Measure theophylline levels |
| Colchicine | No change | No dose change |
| Warfarin | No change | No dose change |

## 8.1 Pregnancy
There are no human data on Xanoridel in pregnancy. In rats given 30 times the human exposure, there were more early losses of pregnancy; at 5 times the human exposure there was no effect. Use Xanoridel in pregnancy only if the benefit justifies the risk to the fetus. Report a pregnancy exposure to Pharma A Medical Safety.

## 8.2 Lactation
It is not known whether xanoridostat passes into human milk. It was found in rat milk at twice the level in rat blood. Weigh the benefit of breastfeeding against the mother's need for Xanoridel.

## 8.3 Females and Males of Reproductive Potential
Xanoridel did not affect fertility in rats at 10 times the human exposure.

## 8.4 Pediatric Use
Safety and effectiveness in patients younger than 18 years have not been established.

## 8.5 Geriatric Use
Of the patients in the trials, 23% were 65 years or older. Exposure was 20% higher in this group, mostly from lower kidney function. Choose the dose by creatinine clearance (see 2.3).

## 8.6 Renal Impairment
Exposure to xanoridostat doubled when creatinine clearance was 15 to 29 mL/min. See 2.3 for the dose by creatinine clearance.

## 8.7 Hepatic Impairment
Exposure rose by 35% in moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B). See 2.4.

## 10 Overdosage
Patients took up to 480 mg in a single dose with no serious effects. There is no antidote. Xanoridostat is removed by hemodialysis only in small amounts.

## 11 Description
Xanoridel tablets contain xanoridostat, a selective xanthine oxidase inhibitor. Each tablet also contains lactose, microcrystalline cellulose and magnesium stearate.

## 12.1 Mechanism of Action
Xanoridostat blocks xanthine oxidase, the enzyme that turns hypoxanthine into xanthine and xanthine into uric acid, so the body makes less uric acid.

## 12.2 Pharmacodynamics
At 80 mg once daily, serum uric acid fell by 47% from baseline after 2 weeks.

## 12.3 Pharmacokinetics
Xanoridostat reaches its peak level 1 to 2 hours after a dose. The half-life is 9 hours, so once daily dosing gives steady levels. Food does not change exposure. It is cleared by the liver (60%) and the kidneys (40%).

## 14.1 XANO-1: Serum Uric Acid Below 6 mg/dL
XANO-1 randomized 1,060 adults with gout and a serum uric acid of 8 mg/dL or more to Xanoridel 80 mg, Xanoridel 160 mg or placebo for 26 weeks.
| Treatment arm | Patients | Serum uric acid below 6 mg/dL at week 26 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Xanoridel 80 mg | 420 | 64% |
| Xanoridel 160 mg | 420 | 79% |
| Placebo | 220 | 2% |

## 14.2 XANO-2: Comparison With Allopurinol
XANO-2 compared Xanoridel 80 mg with allopurinol 300 mg in 760 adults for 52 weeks. At week 52, 61% of patients on Xanoridel and 39% on allopurinol had a serum uric acid below 6 mg/dL.

## 14.3 XANO-CV: Cardiovascular Safety
XANO-CV followed 6,190 adults with gout and cardiovascular disease for a median of 32 months. See 5.2 for the results.

## 16.1 How Supplied
Bottles of 30 tablets: 20 mg (NDC 50199-401-30), 40 mg (NDC 50199-402-30) and 80 mg (NDC 50199-403-30).

## 16.2 Storage and Handling
Store at 20 to 25 °C. Keep the bottle closed to protect from moisture.

## 17.1 Patient Counseling Information
Tell patients to stop Xanoridel and call their doctor at the first sign of a rash; to keep taking Xanoridel during a gout flare; and to get help at once for chest pain, shortness of breath or sudden weakness on one side.

## 17.2 Reporting Suspected Adverse Reactions
Report suspected adverse reactions to Pharma A Medical Safety, or to FDA MedWatch.
